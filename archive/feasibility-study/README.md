# Automated Industrial Garment Measurement: Greenfield Feasibility & Metrology Investigation

## Executive Summary & Navigation Guide

This repository contains an exhaustive, first-principles metrological investigation into the feasibility of automatically measuring factory-produced garments for industrial quality control (QC). 

It combines independent research across **optical metrology, textile physics, computer vision, and quality engineering** with a **rigorous critical evaluation of prior research artifacts (including documentation generated by another AI researcher, Claude, and the previous engineering team's €15k setup)**.

### The Business Context & Problem
In commercial apparel manufacturing, manual quality inspection takes **$45\text{ to }60\text{ seconds}$ per garment**. At that speed, checking every garment in a production lot is impossible, forcing factories to rely on statistical sampling under **AQL (ISO 2859-1)**. Consequently, uninspected defective garments regularly escape into shipping containers, resulting in costly buyer chargebacks and returns. Furthermore, human inspection is highly inconsistent due to variations in positioning, tensioning, and visual landmark interpretation.

The central inquiry is:
> **Can an automated system measure a broad spectrum of production garments (knit tops, woven tops, bottoms) rapidly, repeatedly, and accurately enough to reduce dependence on manual tape inspection and support broad industrial quality control?**

### Key Findings at a Glance

1. **The Binding Bottleneck is Physical and Definitional, Not Sensor Resolution:**
   The fundamental limitation in garment measurement is **not** optical resolution or camera sensor noise. Modern 2D industrial sensors easily achieve $<0.2\text{ mm/pixel}$ across a $1.2\text{ m} \times 1.6\text{ m}$ field. The true limiting factors are:
   - **Definitional Uncertainty ($u_A$):** Vague, text-based specifications in tech packs (e.g., "chest width 2.5 cm below armhole" or "SNP to hem") leave ambiguous datum axes and feature boundaries, causing $1\text{ mm to }3\text{ mm}$ of inter-expert disagreement on a static garment.
   - **Garment State Variability ($u_B$):** Viscoelastic fabric relaxation, handling-induced in-plane shear, table friction hysteresis (where friction cannot sustain elastic recovery tension), seam roll, and out-of-plane wrinkles (where even a $1\text{ mm}$ ridge introduces a $2.7\text{ mm}$ projected shortening error) induce $1.5\text{ mm to }>4\text{ mm}$ of variation between independent lays of the same garment.
   - **Multi-POM Tolerance Stack-Up:** In a 15-POM tech pack, marginal measurement errors compound exponentially. An individual POM false reject rate of just $4\%$ causes a **$45.8\%$ lot-level garment false rejection rate** if evaluated independently!

2. **Critical Evaluation of Claude's Research Artifact (Chapter 00):**
   While Claude correctly established the metrological framework (ISO 18890, ISO 10579, ISO 17450), its physical and operational proposals contain severe engineering flaws:
   - **Backlight Transmission Table (A2) Fails on Opaque Fabrics:** Claude assumed backlighting is universal. In reality, opaque textiles (14oz denim, black fleece, coated twill) transmit zero light ($T < 0.01\%$), rendering internal seams completely invisible. Furthermore, backlighting conflates front and back hemlines on curved or dropped tails into a single silhouette, and induces optical forward-scatter diffraction.
   - **Perforated Vacuum Table (A6) Fails on Finished Garments:** Claude assumed vacuum tables from cutting rooms transfer to garments. In reality, cutting rooms seal dense fabric stacks under an *airtight plastic overlay film*. A finished sewn garment is highly porous; air rushes through the open weave, collapsing the pressure differential ($\Delta P < 0.08\text{ kPa}$) and causing hole dimpling distortion.
   - **Cycle Time & Throughput Illusion:** Claude's stationary manual lay architectures take $35\text{–}50\text{ seconds}$ per garment—saving almost no labor time over the $45\text{–}60\text{ second}$ manual baseline!
   - **Premature Dismissal of 3D Metrology:** Claude discarded 3D sensing entirely, ignoring that monocular 2D cameras suffer $2\text{–}8\text{ mm}$ of perspective parallax error across garment thickness relief. Calibrated **Multi-Camera Stereo Photogrammetry (€1.5k)** is mathematically required to orthorectify 2D dimensions.

3. **Autopsy of the Previous Team's Approach (€15k Zivid + Gemini Synthetic Data):**
   - **Zivid 2+ 3D Camera:** Spatial resolution ($0.44\text{ mm/pixel}$) is coarser than a €800 24 MP sensor; datasheet dimensional trueness ($\pm 0.35\%\text{ to }0.60\%$) introduces $2.5\text{ mm to }4.2\text{ mm}$ of scale bias; and calculating geodesic arc lengths across raw depth data **systematically inflates length by $+26.6\%$** due to depth noise ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta s] \approx a(1 + \sigma_z^2/a^2)$).
   - **Synthetic Gemini / Nano Banana Diffusion Data:** Generative models do not understand projective geometry or sewing pattern topology (Sarkar et al., CVPR 2024). Annotations represented subjective visual guesses with zero metric truth.
   - **Domain Gap & Green Sheet:** Chroma reflections corrupted sub-pixel edge gradients; RMBG downsampled to $1024 \times 1024$ ($1.17\text{ mm/px}$), blurring boundaries.
   - **Keypoint Downsampling & Snapping:** $4\times$ strided heatmaps quantized features into $12\text{–}19\text{ mm}$ cells. Heuristic post-hoc snapping caused **shrinkage toward nominal**, masking true factory defects.

4. **The Real-World Solution: The Two-Tier Industrial Architecture:**
   - **Tier 1 (S-Inline Sentinel):** Continuous conveyor line-scan (Architecture A3) measuring 3 to 5 critical drift POMs in **$5\text{ to }8\text{ seconds}$** for $100\%$ of production units, providing real-time Statistical Process Control.
   - **Tier 2 (S-AQL Collaborative):** Calibrated Dual-Camera Stereo Photogrammetry (Architecture A4-Stereo) paired with touch-screen human semantic confirmation (Architecture A7) measuring full 15-POM spec sheets in **$25\text{ to }35\text{ seconds}$** for lot acceptance audits.
   - **Economics:** Total hardware capex is **€5,000 to €8,000**—less than half the price of the previous €15k setup, while achieving higher planar resolution and zero depth noise inflation.

### Epistemic Conventions and Evidence Classification

Every substantive claim throughout this research is explicitly tagged with an epistemic label:
- **`[E]` Established Fact:** Supported by an authoritative primary source, international standard (ISO, OIML, VDA, AIAG), peer-reviewed journal, or manufacturer specification that was directly examined.
- **`[I]` Engineering Inference:** Deduced from physical first principles, projective geometry, classical mechanics, or mathematical error propagation. Assumptions and derivations are fully laid out.
- **`[H]` Hypothesis:** A plausible physical or algorithmic proposition that requires empirical validation via the Stage S0–S7 experimental protocol.
